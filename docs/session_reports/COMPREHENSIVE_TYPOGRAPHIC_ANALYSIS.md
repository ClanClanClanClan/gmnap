# Comprehensive Typographic Rules Analysis for GMNAP v7

## Overview
To create a truly authoritative bibliographic system, we must understand and implement ALL typographic conventions across every language/region GMNAP covers. This analysis maps the complete scope.

## Regional Coverage Analysis

### A-Group: Latin Script Regions

#### A1: Anglo-sphere (English variants)
**Countries**: US, UK, Canada, Australia, New Zealand, Ireland, South Africa
**Key Rules**:
- **NBSP**: Generally NOT used in English typography
- **Quotation marks**: "double" primary, 'single' secondary (US) vs 'single' primary, "double" secondary (UK)
- **Punctuation spacing**: Single space after periods (modern), no space before punctuation
- **Abbreviations**: Mr., Dr., vs Mr, Dr (UK variant)
- **Numbers**: 1,000.50 (US) vs 1,000·50 or 1 000.50 (some variants)
- **Initials**: J. R. R. Tolkien (spaces after periods)

#### A2: Western Europe (Romance + Germanic)
**Countries**: France, Germany, Italy, Spain, Portugal, Netherlands, Belgium

##### French Typographic Rules:
- **NBSP before**: : ; ! ? » (high punctuation)
- **NBSP after**: « (opening guillemet)  
- **NBSP around**: – (em dash in dialogue)
- **Units**: 25 km, 14 h 30, 3 °C (NBSP before units)
- **Numbers**: 1 000,50 (thin space thousands, comma decimal)
- **Quotes**: « Bonjour » with NBSP
- **Abbreviations**: M. Dupont, Mme Martin (periods required)

##### German Typographic Rules:
- **NBSP**: Limited use, mainly with units
- **Units**: 25 km, 14 Uhr (NBSP before units)
- **Numbers**: 1.000,50 (period thousands, comma decimal)  
- **Quotes**: „Guten Tag" (low-high quotes)
- **Abbreviations**: Prof. Dr. Schmidt
- **Special**: ß handling, Umlaut capitalization rules

##### Italian Typographic Rules:
- **NBSP**: Before : ; ! ? in formal writing
- **Units**: 25 km (NBSP before units)
- **Numbers**: 1.000,50
- **Quotes**: «Ciao» or "Ciao"
- **Abbreviations**: Dott. Rossi, Prof.ssa Bianchi

##### Spanish Typographic Rules:
- **NBSP**: Before ¿ ¡ in questions/exclamations
- **Units**: 25 km (NBSP before units)
- **Numbers**: 1.000,50 or 1 000,50
- **Quotes**: «Hola» or "Hola"
- **Special**: Inverted punctuation ¿Qué? ¡Hola!

##### Portuguese Typographic Rules:
- **Similar to Spanish** but regional variations (Brazil vs Portugal)
- **Units**: 25 km (NBSP)
- **Numbers**: 1.000,50 (Portugal) vs 1,000.50 (Brazil often)

##### Dutch Typographic Rules:
- **NBSP**: Limited, mainly units
- **Units**: 25 km
- **Numbers**: 1.000,50
- **Quotes**: "Hallo" or 'Hallo'

### B-Group: Slavic Script Regions

#### B1: East Slavic (Cyrillic)
**Countries**: Russia, Ukraine, Belarus

##### Russian Typographic Rules:
- **NBSP between initials**: А. С. Пушкин, И. В. Сталин
- **NBSP after abbreviations**: т. е., и т. д., и т. п.
- **NBSP with units**: 25 км, 14 ч, 3 °C
- **NBSP with years**: 1985 г., XIX в.
- **Numbers**: 1 000,50 (thin space thousands, comma decimal)
- **Quotes**: «Привет» (guillemets) or „Привет" (low-high)
- **Em dash**: — (em dash for dialogue, requires spaces)

##### Ukrainian Typographic Rules:
- **Similar to Russian** with variations
- **NBSP patterns**: Same as Russian
- **Quotes**: «Привіт» primarily

##### Belarusian Typographic Rules:
- **Similar to Russian/Ukrainian**

#### B2: South Slavic + Central Europe
**Countries**: Poland, Czech Republic, Slovakia, Croatia, Serbia, Slovenia, Hungary

##### Polish Typographic Rules:
- **NBSP**: Limited use, mainly units
- **Units**: 25 km (NBSP)
- **Numbers**: 1 000,50 (space thousands, comma decimal)
- **Quotes**: „Cześć" (low-high quotes)
- **Special**: Diacritics ą, ć, ę, ł, ń, ó, ś, ź, ż

##### Czech Typographic Rules:
- **NBSP with units**: 25 km
- **Numbers**: 1 000,50
- **Quotes**: „Ahoj"
- **Special**: Diacritics á, č, ď, é, ě, í, ň, ó, ř, š, ť, ú, ů, ý, ž

### C-Group: Arabic Script Regions

#### C2: Persian/Tajik
**Countries**: Iran, Afghanistan, Tajikistan

##### Persian Typographic Rules:
- **RTL script considerations**
- **NBSP**: Context-dependent with Arabic numerals
- **Numbers**: ۱۲۳۴ (Persian digits) vs 1234 (Arabic digits)
- **Punctuation**: ؟ (Arabic question mark), ؛ (Arabic semicolon)
- **Quotes**: «سلام» (guillemets adapted for RTL)

#### C3: Arabic Levant/Nile  
**Countries**: Egypt, Syria, Lebanon, Jordan, Palestine

##### Arabic Typographic Rules:
- **RTL script**
- **NBSP with Arabic numerals** in mixed text
- **Punctuation spacing**: ، (Arabic comma), ؟ ؛
- **Numbers**: ١٢٣٤ (Eastern Arabic) vs 1234 (Western Arabic)
- **Quotes**: «مرحبا» or "مرحبا"

#### C4: Arabic Gulf
**Countries**: Saudi Arabia, UAE, Kuwait, Qatar, Bahrain, Oman

##### Gulf Arabic Rules:
- **Similar to C3** with regional variations
- **Mixed script handling** (Arabic + English names common)

### D-Group: South Asian Regions

#### D1: South Asia Hindi Belt
**Countries**: India (Hindi regions), Nepal

##### Hindi/Devanagari Rules:
- **NBSP considerations** with mixed Devanagari/Latin
- **Numbers**: ०१२३ (Devanagari) vs 1234 (Arabic)
- **Punctuation**: । (danda), ॥ (double danda)
- **Mixed scripts**: English names in Devanagari context

### E-Group: East Asian Regions

#### E1: Sinophone Mainland
**Countries**: China, Taiwan, Singapore (Chinese)

##### Chinese Typographic Rules:
- **No spaces** in traditional Chinese typography
- **Punctuation**: 。，！？；： (full-width punctuation)
- **Mixed script**: Chinese + English names
- **Numbers**: 一二三 vs 1234
- **Quotes**: 「你好」 or "你好" (full-width)
- **Special**: Traditional vs Simplified characters

#### E3: Japan
**Countries**: Japan

##### Japanese Typographic Rules:
- **No spaces** in traditional Japanese
- **Mixed scripts**: Hiragana + Katakana + Kanji + Latin
- **Punctuation**: 。、！？ (full-width)
- **Numbers**: 一二三 vs １２３４ (full-width) vs 1234
- **Quotes**: 「こんにちは」
- **Special**: Vertical vs horizontal text considerations

### G-Group: Latin America

#### G1: Latin America
**Countries**: Mexico, Brazil, Argentina, Colombia, etc.

##### Latin American Rules:
- **Spanish variants**: Similar to European Spanish with regional differences
- **Portuguese (Brazil)**: Different from European Portuguese
- **Indigenous influences**: Mixed with Spanish/Portuguese
- **Regional punctuation variations**

## Cross-Cutting Concerns

### 1. Mixed Script Handling
Many regions use **multiple scripts simultaneously**:
- **Arabic regions**: Arabic + Latin (for foreign names)
- **East Asian**: Chinese/Japanese + Latin
- **South Asian**: Local script + Latin
- **Requirements**: Consistent spacing rules across script boundaries

### 2. Bibliographic Conventions
Academic naming may differ from general typography:
- **Author names**: May preserve original spacing/punctuation
- **Transliteration standards**: ISO, ALA-LC, etc.
- **Historical names**: May use archaic conventions

### 3. Unicode Complexity
- **Combining characters**: Diacritics, marks
- **Normalization**: When to apply NFKC vs preserve
- **Bidirectional text**: RTL + LTR mixing
- **Zero-width characters**: Proper vs improper use

### 4. Regional Variations Within Languages
- **French**: France vs Quebec vs African variants
- **Spanish**: Spain vs Mexico vs Argentina
- **Arabic**: Regional dialectical differences
- **English**: US vs UK vs Australian conventions

## Implementation Challenges

### 1. Rule Conflicts
What happens when:
- Source uses incorrect typography but is "established"?
- Regional variants conflict (European vs American author)?
- Academic standards conflict with national standards?

### 2. Context Sensitivity
Many rules depend on:
- **Document type**: Academic vs popular
- **Historical period**: Rules change over time
- **Mixed languages**: Which rules take precedence?

### 3. Performance Impact
Implementing all rules would require:
- **Complex pattern matching** for each language
- **Context-aware processing** (looking ahead/behind)
- **Multiple passes** for different rule types
